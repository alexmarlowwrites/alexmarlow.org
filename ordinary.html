Ordinary

Collection I

I have always wanted children.

That motherly instinct has always been there, almost like a deep yearning, a little empty space in my heart that I assumed would eventually be filled.

Having children was something I thought I would have to wait for. I would find a good man, settle down, get married, and then, after a few years, have a baby. That was how I imagined it happening.

No nineteen-year-old girl goes out looking to become a stepmom.

I certainly didn't.

Then my husband fell into my life, and I knew I couldn't let him go.

So here I am. Twenty years old, married, and a mom to a very rambunctious three-year-old.

Our little family is my dream. I genuinely wouldn't rather be anywhere else in the world. But sometimes it's hard when other people don't see it that way.

It's the comments people feel entitled to throw your way.

"You're wasting your twenties."

"You got married too young."

"He isn't really yours, though."

"You'll know what it means when you have a baby of your own."

I will never understand how people can say things like that so casually. They don't know what it feels like to hear someone reduce a child you love to a biological technicality.

Because the truth is, I know he isn't biologically mine.

And I have never wanted to pretend that he is.

His mother is his mother. She has always been, and she always will be. I want that for him. I want him to have his mom. I want him to know where he came from and to love her without ever feeling like he has to choose between us.

When I first became a real part of his life, though, she was worried that I was trying to replace her.

And I understood why.

If I were in her position, I think I would have been scared too.

The last thing I wanted was to take something away from her. But somewhere in trying so hard to make sure I wasn't replacing her, I became afraid that there wouldn't be a place for me at all.

Because I didn't want her place.

I wanted my own.

I wanted to be someone he could run to.

Someone he could trust.

Someone he could love.

Someone who could love him back without constantly reminding myself that I was only his stepmom.

I didn't need to be his only mom.

I just wanted to be allowed to be one of them.

And somewhere along the way, without realizing it, I became a mother.

Not all at once. There wasn't one dramatic moment when I woke up and suddenly knew what motherhood was. It happened quietly, in all the little moments I didn't know would matter so much.

Some of my favorite moments with him happen at bedtime and when he first wakes up. Those are the most vulnerable, sacred moments to me. There are no distractions. No toys everywhere, no cartoons playing, no puppy trying to get our attention.

Just us.

Lately, he has started asking if we can "talk for ten minutes" before we go to sleep.

I let him pick the topic, and we talk. Sometimes it's something completely ridiculous. Sometimes I'm amazed by the things that go through his little head. I love our conversations.

When he falls asleep, he has to be touching me. He likes to be the little spoon, curled up against me, and somehow he can't quite settle down unless he knows I'm there.

And every morning, he finds me.

Not my husband.

Not whoever else happens to be there.

Me.

He'll wake up, find me, and give me that half-asleep smile that I don't think I will ever get tired of seeing.

I always ask him the same questions.

Did you sleep good?

Did you have a dream?

Can you tell me what happened?

It's our routine.

Sometimes motherhood is less profound than people make it sound.

Sometimes it is holding a sleepy three-year-old while trying to use the bathroom because apparently I am not allowed to have personal space anymore.

Sometimes it is cleaning a muddy little boy after he has spent the afternoon playing outside.

Sometimes it is going worm hunting even though he is a little scared of bugs.

Sometimes it is sitting beside him playing Minecraft.

Sometimes it is letting him "help" me cook, even when I know it would be much faster if I just did it myself.

Sometimes he wants to help me clean. Sometimes he tries to help me discipline our puppy, although we are still working on that one.

These things probably don't look like motherhood from the outside.

To me, they are.

The other day, I told him he was the best son I could ask for.

His face lit up.

"I am the best??"

I told him yes, of course he was.

Then he looked at me and told me that I was the best mommy.

He has called me Mom before, but something about that moment felt different.

Maybe it was hearing him say it so confidently.

Maybe it was knowing that he understands I am not his biological mother, but somehow, I am still his mommy too.

I sobbed.

And then, because he is the sweetest little boy, he hugged me and made sure I was okay.

A three-year-old comforting me while I cried over being called his mommy.

I don't think I will ever forget it.

And I think that was the moment something finally clicked.

I had spent so much time trying to explain my place in his life to other people that I had forgotten to look at the person who mattered most.

He already knew what I was to him.

Maybe I had been asking the wrong question.

I kept wondering whether I was allowed to call myself his mother. Whether other people would think I had earned that title. Whether loving him as deeply as I do was somehow crossing a line that wasn't mine to cross.

But when I look at our life together, I don't see a line.

I see mornings.

Bedtime conversations.

Sleepy smiles.

Mud.

Worms.

Minecraft.

Cooking.

Cleaning.

Little arms wrapped around me.

A three-year-old who wants to be my little spoon.

A little boy who looks for me when he wakes up.

I see a child who trusts me enough to tell me about his dreams and loves me enough to make sure I'm okay when I cry.

Maybe I didn't become a mother in the way I always imagined I would.

Maybe I didn't carry him.

Maybe I didn't give birth to him.

But motherhood has never only been about biology.

It's about showing up. It's about loving a child even when it's hard. It's about worrying when they're sick, celebrating their little victories, comforting them when they're hurting, listening to their stories even when they make absolutely no sense, and choosing them over and over again.

And I do.

I chose him.

I choose him every day.

For a long time, I thought I needed other people to understand that.

I don't anymore.

Because I know what he means to me.

I know the love I have for him.

And I know that somehow, the little boy I never expected to become a mother to found his way into my life and filled a space in my heart that I had been waiting for my entire life.

Maybe motherhood wasn't something I suddenly became.

Maybe it was something we built together, one ordinary day at a time.

He will forever and always be my first baby.
