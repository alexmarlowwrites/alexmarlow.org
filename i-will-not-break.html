I Will Not Break

Content Warning: Sexual assault, trauma, substance use, family conflict

Maybe you thought I wouldn't remember. Maybe you thought I wouldn't tell anyone. Maybe you thought I would spend the rest of my life carrying what you did in silence.

You were wrong.

I remember.

I remember what you did. I remember what you took from me. I remember the things you said, the things you didn't say, and the things you made me believe about myself. I remember every person who hurt me, every person who betrayed me, every person who decided my pain wasn't real enough to matter. I remember all of it.

And I'm still here.

When I was ten, my father sexually assaulted me. At fifteen, I started using drugs to numb the pain. At sixteen, my boyfriend raped me. At eighteen, an ex-boyfriend stalked me. At nineteen, a friend drugged me. For years, I have also been in constant conflict with my parents, fighting over everything, nothing, and everything again.

Those are the facts. Five ages. Five years of my life. Five things I never should have had to survive. Written out like that, it almost looks simple, but it wasn't. Those aren't just numbers on a timeline. They are years of my life that I don't get back. They are experiences that changed the way I see people, relationships, sex, trust, family, and safety.

They taught me to question people's motives. They taught me to guard my heart. They taught me to hesitate before giving someone all of me. They taught me that sometimes the people who are supposed to make you feel safe are the very people who teach you why you need to be afraid.

And I'm angry about that.

I'm fucking furious.

I'm angry that things other people chose to do became things I have to carry. I'm angry that I learned to be suspicious before I learned to feel safe. I'm angry that trust isn't always something I can give freely. I'm angry that I have to think about things other people never have to think about. I'm angry that my past follows me into relationships it has no business being a part of. I'm angry that I have spent so much of my life trying to understand things I never should have had to experience in the first place.

I am angry because I should have been protected.

I wasn't.

And surviving doesn't erase what happened. I survived, but that doesn't mean it didn't hurt. It doesn't mean it didn't change me. It doesn't mean I walked away untouched. It means I am still here despite everything that tried to convince me I wouldn't be.

There is a part of this story that is harder to admit than the anger: I love my father.

I also hate what he did to me.

Both are true.

I wish they weren't. I wish I could make it simple. I wish loving someone meant they could never hurt you, and I wish being hurt by someone meant you could simply stop loving them entirely. But people aren't that simple. Love doesn't erase harm, and harm doesn't always erase love. Sometimes you can look at someone and remember every good thing about them while also knowing they did something unforgivable to you. Sometimes love and hatred exist in the same person, at the same time, and carrying that contradiction is exhausting.

I think about it even more when I think about having a daughter someday.

I've dreamed about having a daughter for as long as I can remember. I picture a little girl with my eyes, running through my house, asking me questions I don't know how to answer, crawling into my bed when she's scared. I picture brushing her hair, holding her when she cries, watching her become her own person. I want that life so badly.

But underneath that dream is a fear that makes my stomach turn.

How could I ever trust my father around her?

I want her to know her grandfather. I want her to love him. I want her to have happy memories with him. I don't want my daughter to inherit my fear simply because I was never given the chance to have a normal relationship with my own father. But I also know that the last thing I will ever do is gamble her safety for someone else's comfort.

I won't.

If protecting my child means being called cruel, selfish, dramatic, or unforgiving, then call me all of it. I would rather be hated for protecting her than loved for failing her. I know what it feels like when the person who should protect you doesn't, and I will not let that story repeat itself through me.

I can love my father and still have boundaries. I can remember the good and still refuse to ignore the bad. I can care about someone and still decide they don't get access to every part of my life. Love does not require blind trust. I had to learn that the hard way.

My mother is another wound entirely.

She thinks I'm selfish. A liar. A slut.

I never told her about my father. I wasn't ready. But I did tell her about my boyfriend. She adored him. She called him kind and caring, the sort of man any girl would be lucky to have. She saw the version of him he wanted everyone to see. She never knew the real him.

I waited more than a year before telling her what happened. When I finally did, she didn't believe me. She told me it was all in my head.

I don't think she understood what she was asking me to do in that moment. She wasn't just asking me to doubt one memory. She was asking me to doubt myself, and I had already spent years doing that. I had already wondered if I was overreacting, if I was remembering things wrong, if I was somehow responsible, if I had done something differently maybe none of it would have happened.

I didn't need her to fix it. I didn't need her to have the perfect response. I needed her to believe me.

She didn't.

That was the moment I realized something in our relationship had changed permanently. When you finally tell someone the thing you've been carrying alone, you are giving them something fragile. You're trusting them with the part of your story that scares you the most. I gave her that part of me, and she handed it back to me like it wasn't real.

That hurt.

It still does.

These experiences have shaped every relationship I've had since. Of course they have. They have affected the way I love, the way I trust, the way I see sex, the way I protect myself, and the way I imagine my future. There are parts of me that learned to expect betrayal before kindness, danger before safety, and abandonment before permanence.

But I refuse to believe that is all I am.

I am not only what happened to me.

I am not the ten-year-old girl who was hurt by someone who should have protected her. I am not the fifteen-year-old who needed drugs to make the pain quiet. I am not the sixteen-year-old who trusted someone who violated that trust. I am not the eighteen-year-old who was stalked. I am not the nineteen-year-old who was drugged. I am not the daughter who finally told the truth and wasn't believed.

Those things happened to me. They are part of my story. They are not the whole story.

There is so much more of me than the worst things that have happened to me.

I am the person who loves too deeply. I am the person who wants a family. I am the person who still believes in love after everything that has taught me not to. I am the person who wants to build a home that feels safe. I am the person who wants to become a mother and give my children the things I should have had.

And now I'm building that life.

I'm married to the love of my life. I want a family. I want children. I want a home where love doesn't come with conditions and where I don't have to earn the right to be myself. I want a life outside my parents' control. I want to wake up one day and know that the decisions in my life are mine to make.

My parents want their perfect little daughter. Someone they can be proud of. Someone they can show off. Someone who listens, obeys, and never makes them uncomfortable.

But I'm not her.

I haven't been her for a long time.

They tell me I'm an adult, then try to control every decision I make. They threaten to cut me off if I don't obey. They tell me I'm old enough to make my own choices while making it clear they expect those choices to be the ones they would make for me.

I'm tired of it.

I'm tired of carrying other people's expectations like chains. I'm tired of hiding pieces of myself to make other people comfortable. I'm tired of being made to feel guilty for wanting a life that belongs to me. I'm tired of pretending their approval matters more than my own truth.

I've spent enough of my life being afraid. Afraid of what someone might do, afraid of what someone might think, afraid I wouldn't be believed, afraid that trusting someone would give them the opportunity to hurt me, afraid that the things that happened to me would somehow become the definition of who I was.

They don't get to have that much power.

The people who hurt me don't get to decide what kind of woman I become. The people who didn't believe me don't get to decide what I deserve. My parents don't get to decide what my life looks like. My past doesn't get to decide what my future will be.

I do.

I get to decide who I love. I get to decide who I trust. I get to decide what boundaries I have. I get to decide what kind of mother I become. I get to decide what kind of home I build. I get to decide what kind of person I am.

I don't need to erase the past to move forward. I don't need to pretend it didn't hurt. I don't need to forgive everyone who hurt me. I don't need to turn what happened into some beautiful lesson about how trauma made me stronger.

They hurt me.

It changed me.

I survived it.

And now I get to decide what happens next.

That is the difference. They got to choose what they did to me. I get to choose what I do with what remains.

And there is still so much left.

There is a life left. There is love left. There are children I haven't met yet. There are mornings I haven't woken up to. There are memories I haven't made. There is a home I haven't built. There is a version of myself I haven't even met yet.

I refuse to let the people who hurt me have any part in deciding whether I get to experience those things.

I will love the life I am building. I will protect it. I will love my family fiercely. I will become the mother I want to be. I will give my children the safety, trust, and unconditional love that I deserved. I will build a home where fear does not get to run the show.

I will not spend the rest of my life trying to prove that I am worthy of being believed. I will not spend the rest of my life apologizing for surviving badly. I will not spend the rest of my life making myself smaller because other people are uncomfortable with the person I became.

I can be angry and still love. I can be afraid and still move forward. I can carry scars without being defined by them. I can remember without letting the memories control every decision I make. I can fall apart and put myself back together.

I can bend. I can stretch. I can adapt.

But I will not break.

Because I have already survived things I never should have had to survive.

And I'm still here.

There were people who hurt me, people who betrayed me, people who didn't believe me, people who tried to control me, and people who thought they knew who I was and what I deserved.

None of them get the last word.

My father doesn't get the last word. The people who hurt me don't get the last word. The people who didn't believe me don't get the last word. My parents don't get the last word. My past doesn't get the last word. My fear doesn't get the last word.

I do.

I have spent enough of my life waiting for permission. Permission to speak. Permission to make my own choices. Permission to be angry. Permission to leave. Permission to become someone different from who other people wanted me to be.

I'm done waiting.

I am allowed to be angry. I am allowed to protect myself. I am allowed to choose my own life. I am allowed to love who I love. I am allowed to become a mother. I am allowed to be happy. I am allowed to be more than what happened to me.

And I am allowed to say this without making it pretty.

Fuck you.

Fuck every person who thought they could take pieces of me and leave me empty. Fuck every person who thought I would stay silent. Fuck every person who thought what they did would be the thing that defined me forever.

You don't get to decide who I am.

You don't get to decide what I deserve.

You don't get to decide how my story ends.

You already took enough.

You're not taking the rest.

I am tired, but I am alive. I am angry, but I am still capable of love. I am wounded, but I am still whole. I have scars, but they are mine.

And this life is mine too.

Every person who hurt me gets to live with what they did.

I get to live with what I survived.

There is a difference.

I will take every piece of myself that I thought I had lost and carry it with me into the life I'm building. I will take the little girl I was, the teenager who was trying desperately to numb herself, the young woman who wasn't believed, and every version of me that kept getting back up.

I will not leave them behind.

They deserve to come with me.

Because they survived too.

And after everything, I am still here. Still loving, still dreaming, still building, still becoming, still mine.

You thought I wouldn't remember.

You thought I wouldn't tell anyone.

You thought I would spend the rest of my life carrying what you did in silence.

You were wrong.

I remember.

And I'm still here.

You tried to break me.

You didn't.

And you never fucking will.
